**Short answer:** Choose a real decision by someone senior (a date, a design, a priority) where you had a reason to disagree. Show that you first understood their goal, then raised it directly and privately with evidence and an alternative, and accepted the decision either way.

Being overruled and committing fully is as good a story as being right, sometimes better. Finish with the result and what you learned about raising concerns.

## What they are checking

- **Courage:** you speak up when it matters.
- **Respect and timing:** in the right place, early, not in public to score points.
- **Evidence:** risk, cost and options, in terms leaders care about.
- **Disagree and commit:** no quiet resistance afterwards.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** the decision, who made it (role only), the stakes.
2. **Their reason:** stated fairly, as they would say it.
3. **Your concern:** one clear risk, with evidence.
4. **Action:** asked questions first, talked one to one, offered options, named who decides.
5. **Outcome:** what was decided, and how you supported it.
6. **Result and reflection:** what happened, what you changed in yourself.

How to raise it, in four lines:
- "I understand the goal is [goal]. Is that right?"
- "My concern is [risk], because [evidence]. The likely cost is [cost]."
- "I see three options: [A], [B], [C]. I would pick [B]."
- "It is your decision. If we go with [A], I suggest [safeguard]."

## Template

> "At [company], [role, e.g. my director] decided to [decision] because [their reason]. I was responsible for [your part].
>
> I was worried about [risk]. Before saying so, I checked [evidence: data, a small test, an earlier incident], and I asked [questions] to make sure I was not missing something.
>
> I learned that [context you did not know].
>
> I asked for 20 minutes with [role]. I said what I agreed with, showed [evidence], and gave options: [A], [B], with the cost of each.
>
> **[If they changed it:]** They agreed to [compromise], and I took on [the extra work it needed].
>
> **[If they did not:]** They kept the decision because [reason]. I committed: I [what you did to make it succeed], and I put in [safeguard] for the risk.
>
> The result was [outcome with a number if possible]. I learned [lesson], and now I [habit, e.g. bring concerns as options in the first week, not near the deadline]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- A story where the leader is foolish and you were the hero.
- Going over their head first, or complaining to the team.
- Agreeing in the meeting and resisting later.
- No result, or no admission that they had a point.
