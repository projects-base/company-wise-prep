**Short answer:** Pick one or two real achievements outside your day job, say what you did and what came of it, and connect it in one line to a trait the team values: persistence, learning on your own, leadership or giving back. Concrete beats impressive: "I trained for and finished a half marathon" or "I built X in my own time and Y people use it" is better than a vague list of hobbies.

## What they are checking

- **You as a person:** curiosity, discipline, interests beyond the job description.
- **Initiative:** something you chose to do, not something assigned.
- **Follow-through:** you finished it, and can say what came of it.
- **Honesty and calm:** a simple, true story told well.

For an experienced engineer, the question may be phrased "apart from work". Self-driven technical work counts (a side project, open-source contributions, a certification, teaching or mentoring), and so does something entirely non-technical.

## Structure

A short STAR, about 45–60 seconds:

1. **What:** the achievement, in one line.
2. **Why you did it:** what made you start.
3. **What it took:** the effort, a difficulty, how you kept going.
4. **Result:** a fact (finished, ranked, shipped, users, people helped).
5. **Link:** the trait it shows and how it helps at work.

Kinds of story that fit (use only what is true for you): a side project or tool, an open-source fix, a certification earned while working, mentoring juniors or students, a sport or fitness goal, organising an event, volunteering, a creative or musical skill.

## Template

> "Outside work, the thing I'm proudest of is **[achievement]**.
>
> I started because **[reason, e.g. I wanted to learn X properly / a friend asked me to help / I set myself a goal]**. The hard part was **[difficulty, e.g. finding time alongside a full-time job / staying consistent for N months]**, so I **[what you did, e.g. set aside an hour every morning / broke it into small milestones]**.
>
> In the end **[result with a fact: finished in N, N people use it, passed with N%, N students mentored]**.
>
> It taught me **[trait, e.g. how to keep going on something long without external deadlines]**, which is the same thing I rely on at work when **[link, e.g. a migration takes several sprints]**."

Optional second item, one line: "I also **[smaller thing]**."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Listing hobbies ("I like cricket and movies") with no achievement in them.
- Repeating your work or academic results; they asked for something beyond those.
- Exaggerating: a follow-up question will expose it.
- Picking something you can't talk about for two minutes.
- A story with no link back to how you work.
