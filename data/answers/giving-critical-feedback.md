**Short answer:** Tell one real case where you gave someone feedback they did not want to hear. Show that you gave it privately and soon, about a specific behaviour and its impact rather than the person, and that you suggested a way forward. Then say honestly how they reacted and what changed afterwards.

## What they are checking

- **Courage:** you raise problems instead of working around them.
- **Kindness and skill:** specific, private, about behaviour, not personal.
- **Listening:** you asked for their side; maybe you learned something too.
- **Outcome:** did it actually help, and did the relationship survive?

## Structure

STAR + Reflection, with a feedback model inside the Action. A simple one is **SBI**: *Situation* (when), *Behaviour* (what you saw), *Impact* (what it caused), then ask for their view and agree a next step.

Fitting stories for a backend engineer: repeated PRs merged without tests, a teammate dismissive in code reviews, someone missing stand-up commitments that blocked others, a senior's design that ignored an operational risk.

## Template

> "In [context], [teammate's role, no names needed] kept [behaviour, e.g. merging changes to the shared payment module without tests]. It had already caused [impact, e.g. two regressions in staging, and a hotfix].
>
> I asked them for a quick one-to-one rather than raising it in the review thread. I said: 'In the last two sprints, [specific example]. It led to [impact]. I'm worried because [why it matters]. What's making it hard?' It turned out [their reason, e.g. they felt pressure to close tickets fast].
>
> They were [honest reaction, e.g. a bit defensive at first]. We agreed [action, e.g. I'd pair with them on testing the module, and we'd raise the deadline pressure with our lead together].
>
> Afterwards, [result, e.g. their next PRs had tests, and they later helped a new joiner with the same thing]. What I learned was [reflection, e.g. asking 'what's making it hard?' got more than the feedback itself]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Feedback given in public, or only to the manager behind their back.
- Vague feedback ("be more careful").
- A story where the other person was simply wrong and you were simply right.
- Skipping how they took it; the question asks for that explicitly.
- Picking something trivial, like code style.
