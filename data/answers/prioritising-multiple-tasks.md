**Short answer:** Describe a real week with more work than time: the tasks, the criteria you used (impact, deadline, who is blocked, effort), the order you chose, and who agreed to it. Show that you said clearly what would *not* be done, and what happened to each task.

The method matters more than the busy week. Mention one time you changed the order when facts changed.

## What they are checking

- **A repeatable method**, not only hard work.
- **Business sense:** impact decides, not the loudest voice.
- **Communication:** people know early what will wait.
- **Focus:** you finish things and limit work in progress.
- **Flexibility:** you re-plan when something urgent arrives.

## Structure

Method in 30 seconds, then STAR + Reflection.

1. **List everything** in one place, with the requester and the real deadline.
2. **Sort by impact and urgency.** Ask "what breaks, and for whom, if this waits a week?"
3. **Look for blockers:** short tasks that free other people go early.
4. **Estimate roughly,** and cut or split the big ones.
5. **Agree the order** with the manager or product owner when interests conflict.
6. **Tell the people whose work waits,** with a date.
7. **Review daily** in five minutes.

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | do now (production issue) | schedule, and protect the time (design, tests) |
| **Less important** | delegate or make quick | say no, or later |

The box people neglect is "important, not urgent". Say how you protect it.

## Template

> "In [period] I had [task A] due [date], [task B] asked by [role], [task C] which blocked [person], and [ongoing duty, e.g. support].
>
> I listed them with deadline, impact and effort. [Task C] took only [time] and freed [person], so I did it first.
>
> [Task A] affected [customers / date], so it got my best hours. [Task B] could wait until [date].
>
> I checked the order with [manager], and told [requester of B] the same day. I also [delegated / cut scope on] [part].
>
> Half-way, [something changed, e.g. a production issue]. I stopped [task], told [people] the new dates, and returned to it on [day].
>
> In the end [result for each task]. I would now [reflection, e.g. ask for the true deadline earlier]."

Likely follow-ups:
- *Two managers both say theirs is first?* I put both in one conversation with the facts, and let them decide; I do not choose in secret.
- *How do you say no?* "Yes, after [X], on [date]. If it must be sooner, which of these should move?"

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I multitask well" or "I stay late".
- First come, first served as the method.
- Naming a tool (Jira, a to-do app) instead of the thinking.
- Every task finished perfectly; real priorities mean something waited.
