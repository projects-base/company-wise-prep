**Short answer:** Tie your reasons to what Tower actually does and values: engineering for low-latency trading systems where performance and correctness have a direct, measurable effect; engineers who own their systems; and a culture that tries ideas with a proof of concept before adopting them. Then connect it to your own move from framework-level Java backend work to deeper systems-level engineering. Avoid "money" and "HFT is cool".

## What they are checking

- **Real interest** in the domain and the engineering, not just the pay.
- **Research:** you know what Tower is (a quantitative trading firm) and roughly how engineering supports it.
- **Fit:** Tower's stated values are Excellence, Respect, Innovation, Integrity and Teamwork, and it names ownership and calm under pressure for engineers.
- **Commitment:** you understand the bar is high and the pace is fast.

## Structure

Three short reasons plus a closing link, under a minute.

1. **The problem space:** latency, throughput and reliability really matter; feedback is fast and measurable.
2. **The culture:** ownership, small teams, innovation proven by PoC.
3. **Your growth:** from Spring Boot service work toward systems depth (JVM internals, concurrency, networking, memory). Show you are already working on it.
4. **Team link:** if it is a post-trade, platform or shared-services role, say why that part interests you.

## Template

> "Three reasons.
>
> First, the engineering problems. In trading systems, performance and correctness aren't abstract; a few microseconds or one wrong record has a direct cost. After [~5.5 years] building Java backend services, I want to work where [specific interest, e.g. latency and concurrency are the main design constraints].
>
> Second, ownership. From what I've read, engineers at Tower own their systems end to end and new ideas get proved with a proof of concept before they're adopted. That matches how I like to work: [brief example, e.g. on (project), I built a PoC of (X) before we committed to it].
>
> Third, growth. I've been going deeper into [e.g. the Java memory model, lock-free concurrency, JVM tuning], and I want a role where that depth is used every day. [Role / team, e.g. post-trade] fits because [reason, e.g. it is where my experience with correctness-critical backend systems and databases transfers directly]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Money, prestige, or "I love finance" with nothing behind it.
- Claiming trading or HFT experience you don't have.
- Not knowing what team or role you applied for.
- Facts about Tower you can't back up. Check Tower's own careers and engineering pages before the interview.
