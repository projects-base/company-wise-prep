**Short answer:** Share a real piece of critical feedback about something that mattered, say honestly how you felt, then focus on what you did: asked for specifics, made a concrete change, and checked later whether it worked. End with evidence that the change stuck.

## What they are checking

Both Google (Googleyness) and Microsoft (growth mindset) ask this.

- **Openness:** you can hear criticism without getting defensive.
- **Action:** the feedback turned into a change in behaviour.
- **Follow-through:** you checked back and the change lasted.
- **Real weakness:** a meaningful one, not a disguised strength.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **The feedback:** who gave it, in what context, and what it said (quote it if you can).
2. **Your first reaction:** a brief honest line ("my first instinct was to explain why").
3. **Clarify:** you asked for examples to understand it.
4. **Change:** the specific action.
5. **Result:** later feedback or outcome showing it worked.

Fitting kinds of feedback for a backend engineer: PRs too large to review; not communicating status or delays early; designs that skipped operational concerns (monitoring, rollback); talking too technically to non-engineers; taking on too much alone instead of asking for help.

## Template

> "In [review / 1:1 / retro] at [company], [manager / peer / tech lead] told me [feedback, e.g. 'your PRs are hard to review, people put them off']. My first reaction was [honest reaction], but I asked for examples, and they pointed to [specific case].
>
> I realised [insight, e.g. I was bundling refactoring and features in one PR]. I changed [action, e.g. I started splitting work into PRs under ~300 lines, separating refactors, and writing a short description of what to look at first].
>
> A few months later, [result, e.g. review time on my PRs dropped from days to hours, and the same reviewer mentioned it in my review].
>
> Now I [ongoing habit, e.g. ask for feedback at the end of each project instead of waiting for review season]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I've never had negative feedback."
- A strength in disguise ("I care too much about quality").
- Explaining why the feedback was wrong.
- Feedback with no follow-up or result.
- Choosing something that is a red flag for the role (for example, integrity issues).
