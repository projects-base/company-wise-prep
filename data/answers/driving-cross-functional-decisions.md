**Short answer:** Tell a real decision that needed several functions (product, operations, data, security, another engineering team) with different goals, where *you* moved it from stuck to decided. Show how you framed the question, agreed the criteria and the decider, brought evidence, handled the objections, and wrote the decision down.

End with the outcome and whether the decision held. The skill is getting agreement without having authority.

## What they are checking

- **Influence without authority.**
- **Empathy:** you can state each group's goal in its own words.
- **Structure:** criteria, options, an owner, a date.
- **Follow-through:** the decision was carried out, not only agreed.
- **Scope:** for a staff role, several teams and a lasting effect.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection, with the decision process as the Action.

1. **Situation:** the decision, why it was stuck, the cost of waiting.
2. **People:** each function, what it wanted, what it feared.
3. **Frame:** one question, one decider, one date.
4. **Criteria first:** agree what "good" means before comparing options.
5. **Evidence:** data, a prototype, a cost estimate.
6. **One-to-ones before the meeting:** hear objections early and change the proposal.
7. **Decide and record:** a short decision note with the options rejected and why.
8. **Follow up:** owners, dates, a review point, a metric.

A small table you can describe or draw:

| Criterion (agreed) | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| [customer effect] | [ ] | [ ] |
| [effort and date] | [ ] | [ ] |
| [risk and how to undo it] | [ ] | [ ] |

A useful phrase is **"can we undo it?"**: if yes, decide fast; if not, take time and more views.

## Template

> "We had to decide [decision], which affected [function 1], [function 2] and [function 3]. It had been open for [time], and it was costing [cost].
>
> [Function 1] wanted [goal] and worried about [fear]. [Function 2] wanted [goal].
>
> I did not manage any of them; my role was [role].
>
> I wrote one page with the question, the decider ([role]) and a date. I spoke to each person alone, and from that the criteria became [criteria].
>
> I gathered [evidence]. It showed [finding], which was not what I had expected because [reason].
>
> [Function 2] still objected to [point], so I changed the proposal: [change]. We met for [time], and [decider] chose [option].
>
> I recorded it, agreed owners, and checked after [period]. The result was [outcome with a number].
>
> Next time I would [improvement, e.g. bring operations in a week earlier]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Only engineers in the story: that is not cross-functional.
- "I convinced everyone" with no objections and no change to your proposal.
- A decision by endless consensus with no decider.
- Hiding the trade-off: who lost something, and how you dealt with it.
- Stopping at the meeting, with no outcome.
