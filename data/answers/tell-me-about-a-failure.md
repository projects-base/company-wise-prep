**Short answer:** Choose a real case where you did not deliver what you committed to: a missed deadline, a feature that did not work as promised, or a bug you shipped. State it plainly, own your part, explain how you limited the damage and communicated, and show the specific change you made so it did not happen again.

## What they are checking

At a director round, this tests **accountability** and **maturity**.

- **Ownership:** "I", not "the team" or "the requirements".
- **Damage control:** what you did as soon as you knew.
- **Communication:** you told people early, not at the deadline.
- **Systemic learning:** a lasting change, ideally one the team adopted too.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection, about 2 minutes.

1. **Commitment:** what you were supposed to deliver, and to whom.
2. **The miss:** what actually happened, plainly.
3. **Why:** your part first, then any context (briefly).
4. **Response:** how you handled it once you knew: told stakeholders, recovered what you could.
5. **Change:** the process or habit you changed.
6. **Proof:** a later case where the change worked.

Realistic misses for a backend engineer: an estimate that was badly wrong because of an unknown legacy dependency; a release that caused an incident; a migration that slipped; a performance target not met.

## Template

> "At [company], I committed to [deliverable] by [date] for [stakeholder]. I missed it by [amount / way].
>
> The main reason was mine: [e.g. I estimated without checking how the legacy module handled X, and it needed a rewrite]. [Context, briefly, if relevant.]
>
> When I realised, on [when], I [response, e.g. told my lead and the PM the same day, with a revised date and a smaller version we could ship on the original date]. We shipped [partial scope] on time and the rest [when].
>
> The impact was [honest impact, e.g. the client's rollout moved by a week].
>
> Since then I [change, e.g. do a one-day spike on any unfamiliar module before giving an estimate, and give ranges instead of single dates]. On [later project], that [result, e.g. surfaced a similar issue in week one]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- A fake failure or a disguised success.
- Blaming the PM, QA, or another team.
- A catastrophic story with no ownership or recovery.
- Telling stakeholders only at the deadline.
- No lasting change.
