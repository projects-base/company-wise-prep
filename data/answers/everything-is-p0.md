**Short answer:** When everything is P0, nothing is. I list all the work in one place, rank it by real impact and urgency (customer or revenue impact, deadlines, whether anything is blocked on me), and then take that ranked list to my manager or stakeholders so *they* confirm the order and what gets pushed. I communicate early about what will slip, and I ask for help or scope cuts rather than silently working nights.

## What they are checking

- **Prioritisation method**, not just "I worked harder".
- **Communication:** you make trade-offs visible and get them agreed.
- **Self-management:** you notice overload early and say so.
- **Honesty (the follow-up):** can you admit a time you dropped something, and what you did to repair it?

## Structure

Give the **method**, then a **story** (STAR + Reflection), then answer the follow-up.

Method:
1. **Inventory:** every task, its requester, and its real deadline.
2. **Rank:** impact (who is hurt and how much if it's late), urgency, dependencies, effort. Quick wins that unblock others go early.
3. **Agree:** share the ranked list with the manager; ask "if I can only do two, which two?"
4. **Communicate slips** before the deadline, with a new date.
5. **Reduce load:** delegate, cut scope, or ask for a temporary hand.

## Template

> "During [period, e.g. a release week], I had [N] urgent items: [item A], [item B], [item C], each marked as top priority by [different people].
>
> I wrote them down with impact and deadline, and saw that [A] affected [customers / revenue] while [C] could wait [time]. I took the list to [manager] and we agreed [order]. I told [requester of C] the same day that it would land on [date], and [colleague] picked up [B].
>
> Result: [A] shipped on time, [outcome for B and C]."

**Follow-up (you could not handle it):**

> "Once, on [occasion], I didn't do this. I tried to do all of [tasks] myself, and [what slipped, e.g. a fix I had promised went out two days late]. I told [person] as soon as I realised, apologised, gave a new date and met it. Since then I [habit, e.g. raise overload the moment I have more than two 'urgent' items, and keep a visible priority list]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I just work longer hours." It suggests poor judgement and burnout.
- Deciding priorities alone and surprising stakeholders later.
- A follow-up story with no repair action.
- Claiming you have never dropped anything.
