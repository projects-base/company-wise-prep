**Short answer:** Tell a story where you noticed a teammate struggling, found out the actual cause with a private conversation, and then gave concrete, ongoing support such as pairing, smaller well-defined tasks, or clearer review feedback. Show the measurable improvement, and make clear you respected them and involved the manager only in the right way.

## What they are checking

This maps to Google's emergent leadership: leading without the title.

- **Empathy:** you assumed a cause, not laziness.
- **Diagnosis:** skill gap, unclear expectations, workload, or something personal?
- **Concrete help:** time and structure, not just advice.
- **Judgement:** when it is your job and when it is the manager's.
- **Results:** did they improve?

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** what "underperforming" looked like (missed estimates, bugs, blocked for days).
2. **Diagnosis:** a private, non-judgemental chat. "How's the work going? What's slowing you down?"
3. **Plan:** pick help that fits the cause. Skill gap: pairing, a walkthrough of the codebase, a checklist. Unclear tasks: break tickets down together. Overload: talk to the lead about scope. Personal issues: point them to the manager and give space.
4. **Follow-through** over weeks, not one meeting.
5. **Result and reflection.**

## Template

> "On [team], a [new / junior / transferred] teammate was [symptom, e.g. taking a week on tickets estimated at two days, and PRs came back with many comments]. People were starting to [impact, e.g. route work around them].
>
> I asked them for a coffee and asked what was slowing them down. It turned out [cause, e.g. they didn't know how our Spring modules were wired and were too shy to ask].
>
> I [actions, e.g. did two pairing sessions on the order flow, drew the module diagram, and suggested we split their next ticket into three small PRs]. I also told our lead [what, e.g. that they needed smaller tickets for a few sprints], without making it a complaint.
>
> Over [time], [result, e.g. their PRs went from many comments to a few, and they owned a feature end to end]. What I learned was [reflection]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Calling the person lazy or incompetent.
- Doing their work for them (it hides the problem).
- Going straight to the manager without talking to them first.
- No measurable result.
- Taking all the credit for their improvement.
