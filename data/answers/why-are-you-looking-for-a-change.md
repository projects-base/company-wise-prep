**Short answer:** Make it a "pull" answer, not a "push" answer: talk about what you are moving toward (scope, technology, scale, growth), not what you are escaping. Acknowledge what you've gained in your current role, give one or two forward-looking reasons, and link them to what this company and role offer. If they ask why not move internally, say you explored it and explain honestly, without blaming anyone.

## What they are checking

- That you aren't running from a problem that will follow you (conflict, performance, burnout).
- That your reasons match what this job actually offers, so you'll stay.
- Maturity: can you talk about your employer respectfully?
- For "what do you expect from us": realistic expectations about the role, learning and growth.

## Structure

1. **Appreciation (1 line):** what the current role gave you.
2. **The pull (2-3 lines):** the growth you want next, concrete and professional.
3. **The fit (1-2 lines):** why this role delivers it.
4. **Internal move (if asked):** you looked, and the opportunities you want aren't available there right now.

## Template

> I've had a good **[~5.5]** years at **[company]**. I've grown from **[starting point]** to owning **[area]**, and I learned a lot about **[e.g. building and running Spring Boot services in production]**.
>
> I'm now looking for **[broader scope: e.g. end-to-end full-stack ownership, larger-scale systems, more client-facing work, a wider variety of domains]**. In my current setup, **[neutral fact: the product is mature and mostly in maintenance / the stack is fixed / the team structure limits scope]**.
>
> This role fits that well: **[specific: Java microservices with React, the client domains, the chance to work on X]**.
>
> *(If asked about internal moves)* I did look at **[internal options]**, but **[neutral reason: the projects using the stack I want to grow in aren't open right now / internal moves would keep me in the same domain]**.
>
> *(If asked what you expect)* Meaningful work on **[the stack]**, a team that reviews code seriously, and a clear path to **[senior / lead]** responsibilities.

If money is a real part of it, it's fine to say it once, plainly: "Compensation is a factor too, but the main reason is **[growth reason]**."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Complaining about your manager, team or company. It reads as a future risk.
- "I'm bored" or "for the hike" as the only reason.
- A reason this company can't fix either (for example "I want to work on product" when interviewing at a services firm, unless the role is product work).
- Giving a different reason to every interviewer in the loop. Keep one consistent story.
- Overlong answers. Sixty seconds is plenty.
