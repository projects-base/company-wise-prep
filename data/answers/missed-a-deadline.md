**Short answer:** The best moment to handle a missed deadline is *before* it: the day I believe the date is at risk, I tell the people who depend on it, with options and a recommendation. If it is already missed, I own it, give a new date I can defend, deliver the most valuable part first, and then find the cause so it does not repeat.

Then give a short real example of a date you missed and what you changed afterwards.

## What they are checking

- **Ownership:** no excuses and no blame.
- **Early communication:** no surprises.
- **Learning:** your estimating or planning really changed.

## Structure

It is hypothetical, so give the **method**, then a **story** (STAR + Reflection).

**As soon as it looks at risk:**
1. **Check the facts:** what is left, what blocks it, how sure are you?
2. **Tell the people affected the same day:** status, reason, impact.
3. **Bring options:** less scope, a later date, more help, a different order. Say which you recommend.
4. **Agree one,** with a written new date and check-points.

**If it is already missed:**
1. Say so plainly, and apologise once.
2. Reduce the harm: a workaround, a partial release behind a flag, a manual step.
3. Give a realistic date; missing a second one costs the trust.

**Afterwards:**
- Was it the estimate, the scope, a dependency, an interruption, or asking for help too late?

A message you could send:

> "The [feature] date of [date] is at risk. [Cause], so I now expect [new date].
>
> - Option A: ship [part] on time and the rest on [date].
> - Option B: everything on [date].
>
> I recommend A, because [reason]. Can you confirm by [time]?"

## Template

> "If I missed a deadline, I would first tell [stakeholders] with the impact and a plan, not only the news. I would look at what can still be delivered, agree the new date, and then fix the cause.
>
> It has happened to me. On [project], I had committed to [deliverable] by [date], and [cause, including your own part, e.g. I had not checked a dependency].
>
> I noticed on [day] and told [person] [when]. We agreed to [option], so [users] got [the important part] on time, and the rest came on [date].
>
> Afterwards I [change in how you work]. Since then [evidence that it helped]."

Likely follow-ups:
- *Would you cut tests to make the date?* I cut scope first. If a shortcut is agreed, it is written down with an owner and a date.

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I never miss deadlines."
- "I would work all night" as the whole plan.
- Telling people on the day itself.
- Blaming another team, or the estimate someone else made.
