**Short answer:** For the reasonable demand, pick a stretch request (a tight deadline or a hard goal) that you met, and show how you planned it. For the unreasonable one, show that you did not just comply or refuse: you understood the reason behind it, made the trade-offs visible with facts, offered options, and reached an agreement. Stay respectful about the manager in both stories.

## What they are checking

- **Judgement:** can you tell a hard demand from an unreasonable one? Hard but achievable is reasonable. Unreasonable means it can't be done as asked without hidden cost: quality, safety, people's health, or ethics.
- **Pushing back well:** with data and options, not complaints.
- **Professionalism:** no manager-bashing.
- **Ethics line:** if a demand crosses into something unsafe or dishonest, you escalate.

## Structure

Two short STAR stories.

**Reasonable:** the ask, why it was a stretch, how you planned it, the result.

**Unreasonable:**
1. **Understand:** ask what is driving the demand (a client commitment, a leadership date).
2. **Quantify:** estimate, list risks, show what would have to give.
3. **Offer options:** smaller scope by the date, full scope later, more people, or accept a named risk in writing.
4. **Decide together** and commit.
5. **Escalate** only if it is unsafe or unethical, and tell the manager first.

## Template

> **Reasonable:** "[Manager] asked me to [ask, e.g. deliver an integration two weeks earlier than planned] because [reason]. It was tight but possible. I [plan, e.g. cut a nice-to-have, parallelised testing with QA]. We delivered on [date], and [result]."
>
> **Unreasonable:** "Another time, [manager] asked for [ask, e.g. a full feature in one week, with no time for testing, to ship before a client demo]. I asked what was driving it and learned [reason]. I came back with an estimate and the risks: [e.g. untested payment code in production]. I proposed [option A: a demo build behind a flag, the full release in three weeks] and [option B]. We chose [option], and [result]. The lesson for me was [reflection, e.g. bringing options instead of a 'no' turns a standoff into a planning discussion]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Calling your manager bad or incompetent.
- Saying yes to everything (it sounds like no judgement), or refusing with no alternative.
- Choosing an "unreasonable" story that was simply hard work.
- Not answering both parts.
