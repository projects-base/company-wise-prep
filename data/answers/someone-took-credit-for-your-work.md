**Short answer:** I would first assume it might be a misunderstanding and talk to the person privately: say what I saw, without accusing, and ask how we can make sure contributions are shown accurately. Going forward I would make my work visible by default (design docs, PR descriptions, demo slots). If it keeps happening, I would raise it with the manager as a team-process issue, with facts, not as a personal grievance.

## What they are checking

- **Composure:** you don't react publicly or emotionally.
- **Good faith first:** you check before accusing.
- **Directness:** you address it with the person, not only behind their back.
- **Team focus:** you care that credit is fair for everyone, not just you.
- **Escalation judgement** (the follow-up): when and how to involve the manager.

## Structure

This is often hypothetical. Give your **approach** in steps, add a short **real example** if you have one, then answer the **follow-up**.

1. **Pause:** don't respond in the meeting where it happened. A neutral addition is fine ("Happy to go into the caching part, I built that piece").
2. **Talk privately:** "In the demo, the retry design came across as yours. I built that part. Can we make sure we split credit clearly next time?"
3. **Make work visible:** your name on design docs, clear PR history, offer to present your own parts.
4. **Let it go if it was one-off** and they correct it.

## Template

> "My first step would be a private chat, because often it's not intentional: someone summarises team work and says 'I' out of habit. I'd say: '[specific instance]. I worked on [part]. How can we present it more accurately next time?'
>
> [Optional real example: 'Something similar happened at [company] when [situation]. I [action], and [result].']
>
> **If it keeps happening in a four-person team:** in a team that small, it hurts everyone's trust, not just mine. I'd bring it to the manager as a process issue, with specific examples, and suggest fixes that help everyone: rotating who demos, a written summary of who did what in each sprint review, or using PR and ticket history in performance reviews. I'd tell the person before I raise it, so it isn't a surprise."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Calling them out in front of the team or the client.
- Doing nothing and staying resentful.
- Going straight to the manager on the first instance.
- Making it all about your own recognition.
