**Short answer:** Pick a real professional disappointment where you had some part in the cause, not one that was purely bad luck or someone else's fault. Say briefly what happened, own your share of why, and spend most of the time on what you learned and the habit you changed. End with evidence that the new habit worked later.

## What they are checking

- **Self-awareness:** can you see your own part in an outcome without blaming others?
- **Emotional maturity:** you felt it, but you did not dwell on it or get defensive.
- **Growth:** the lesson turned into a concrete, repeatable behaviour, not a slogan.
- **Honesty:** a small, safe "disappointment" ("I wanted the project to be even better") reads as dodging.

## Structure

Use STAR with a heavy *Result + Reflection*:

1. **Situation** (2 sentences): the project and why it mattered to you.
2. **What went wrong** (2-3 sentences): the outcome you hoped for and the one you got.
3. **Why it happened** (most honest part): the causes, including yours.
4. **What you learned and changed** (longest part): the specific habit.
5. **Proof:** a later situation where the habit paid off.

Good kinds of story for a backend engineer with ~5 years' experience: a feature you built that users barely adopted, a promotion or role you missed, a migration that slipped badly, a design you championed that was later rolled back.

## Template

> "My biggest disappointment was [the outcome], on [project / context]. I had [what you invested or expected, e.g. pushed for this design, worked on it for N months].
>
> What happened was [the result, with a number if you have one]. Looking back, part of it was outside my control, [one external cause, briefly]. But a real part was mine: I [your mistake, e.g. didn't validate the requirement with users early / assumed the downstream team's API would be ready / didn't raise the risk when I first saw it].
>
> It stung, because [why it mattered to you]. What I took from it was [the lesson in one sentence]. Since then I [the concrete habit, e.g. ship a thin slice to real users in the first two weeks / write down dependencies and their owners in the design doc / flag a risk the same day I see it].
>
> On [later project], that habit [the result, e.g. caught a wrong assumption in week one and saved a rework]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Choosing a personal-life disappointment, or one so big it raises red flags.
- Blaming the manager, the team or "the business" for everything.
- A lesson with no behaviour change ("I learned communication is important").
- Sounding bitter, or spending longer on the story than on the learning. Aim for 2 minutes.
