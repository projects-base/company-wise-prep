**Short answer:** Pick one real, work-related thing (release speed, testing, on-call, documentation), say why it exists fairly, and say what it costs. Then spend most of the answer on what *you* have done to improve it and what happened.

Keep people out of it and keep the tone constructive. They are also listening for whether your complaint exists in their company too.

## What they are checking

- **Constructive or bitter:** how you talk about people who are not in the room.
- **Ownership:** you try to change things, not only notice them.
- **Judgement:** you picked something that matters and understand the reasons behind it.
- **Fit:** what you need from a workplace, and whether they can offer it.

## Structure

1. **Something good first:** one honest sentence.
2. **The change:** one topic, specific, about a process or a system.
3. **Why it is like that:** show you understand the constraint.
4. **The cost:** time, quality or customers, with a number if you have one.
5. **What you did:** a proposal, a pilot, a small fix.
6. **Result, or where it is now:** unfinished is fine.
7. **Link:** what you look for next, briefly.

Safe and useful topics (choose one that is true):
- Releases are large and rare; smaller ones would be safer.
- Tests are slow or flaky, so people stop trusting them.
- Knowledge lives in a few heads; few design notes.
- Alerts are noisy, so real ones get missed.
- Engineers hear about user problems late and second-hand.

Topics to leave out: salary, a person, politics, working hours as a complaint, anything confidential.

## Template

> "There is a lot I like, especially [real positive].
>
> If I could change one thing, it would be [process or system]. It is like that because [fair reason, e.g. it made sense when the team was small], but today it costs us [cost].
>
> I did not want only to complain, so I [action, e.g. wrote a one-page proposal and tried it with one service]. [Person or group] was worried about [concern], so I [how you handled it].
>
> So far [result]. [If unfinished: the next step is X, and what is missing is Y.]
>
> It taught me that I work best where [what you value], which is one reason I am interested in [something true about this company]."

**If nothing you tried worked:**

> "I raised it with [role], and the decision was to keep it because [reason]. I understood that, and in my own area I still [small improvement]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- "Nothing, it is perfect." Not believable, and it shows no thinking.
- Criticising your manager or colleagues.
- A problem with no attempt to improve it.
- Something this company also does, without having checked. Read their engineering posts first.
