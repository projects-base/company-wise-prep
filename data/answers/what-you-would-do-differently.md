**Short answer:** Take one piece of work and answer the four parts in order: what I did, how, what I learned, and what I would do differently now. Keep the first two short, because the last two are the point.

Choose one or two changes that really would have altered the result, explain why you did not see them then, and show that you have already worked that way since.

## What they are checking

- **Reflection:** you look back critically without being asked.
- **Growth:** your judgement now is better than it was then.
- **Honesty:** a real weakness in your work, not a polished success.
- **Transfer:** the lesson was used again.

## Structure

CARL works well here: Context, Action, Result, Learning, plus the redo.

1. **What (20 s):** the problem, your role, the size.
2. **How (40 s):** the two or three decisions that mattered.
3. **Result (15 s):** a number, and what was not good enough.
4. **Learned (30 s):** the insight, and the evidence that taught you.
5. **Better now (45 s):** the concrete change, its cost, and what you would keep.
6. **Proof:** where you have applied it since.

Two kinds of "differently" are good together:

- **Technical:** the boundary, the data model, the test strategy, the rollout.
- **Way of working:** asking users earlier, measuring first, splitting the delivery, involving another team.

Prompts to find yours:
- What caused the most rework or the first incident?
- What did we build that nobody used?
- Where did I wait too long before asking for help?

Keep the test of hindsight: **"Was it wrong, or only wrong later?"** A simple design that was outgrown is not a mistake, so say what signal should have told you to change.

## Template

> "On [project] the problem was [problem], and I was responsible for [part].
>
> I [key decision 1] and [key decision 2], because at that time [constraint or assumption].
>
> It worked in that [result with a number], but [what was not good: cost, delay, incident, rework].
>
> I learned [insight]. I saw it when [evidence].
>
> Today I would [change 1], which would have [effect]. I would also [change 2, way of working].
>
> It would cost [trade-off], which I think is worth it because [reason].
>
> I would keep [good decision], because [reason].
>
> I have used this since: on [later work] I [applied it], and [result]."

Likely follow-ups:
- *Why didn't you do that at the time?* Name the missing knowledge or the pressure, without blaming anyone.
- *Did you go back and fix it?* Say yes with the result, or no with the reason.

## Mistakes to avoid

- "Nothing, it went well."
- "I would use [newer technology]" with no problem that it solves.
- Blaming the deadline, the manager or the team.
