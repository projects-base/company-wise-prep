**Short answer:** Give two strengths and one or two weaknesses from each viewpoint, and make the three views consistent with each other. Your manager sees outcomes and reliability, your peers see collaboration and code, and you see your own habits. Back every strength with a short example and every weakness with what you are doing about it.

## What they are checking

- **Self-awareness:** do you know how others experience you?
- **Consistency:** the three views should overlap. Totally different lists look invented.
- **Honesty:** real weaknesses, each with an improvement plan.
- **Evidence:** ideally things a manager or peer has actually said in reviews or feedback.

## Structure

The prompt says *write down*, so expect a table or list, then a discussion.

| View | Focus | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Manager | delivery, ownership, communication up | 2 strengths + 1 weakness |
| Peers | reviews, help, collaboration | 2 strengths + 1 weakness |
| Self | habits, growth areas | 2 strengths + 1-2 weaknesses |

Prepare by re-reading your past performance reviews and peer feedback. Use their words where you can.

## Template

> **Manager:**
> - Strength: [e.g. reliable delivery of backend features, example: (feature) shipped on time]
> - Strength: [e.g. owns production issues, example: (incident)]
> - Weakness: [e.g. could communicate risks earlier]. *Working on it by:* [action]
>
> **Peers:**
> - Strength: [e.g. thorough, helpful code reviews]
> - Strength: [e.g. go-to person for (Spring/JPA/area)]
> - Weakness: [e.g. sometimes takes on too much alone instead of asking for help]. *Working on it by:* [action]
>
> **Self:**
> - Strength: [e.g. debugging complex issues methodically]
> - Strength: [e.g. learning new areas quickly, example]
> - Weakness: [e.g. less confident presenting designs to large groups]. *Working on it by:* [action]
>
> Then say: "The common thread is [link, e.g. I'm strong on execution and depth, and my growth area is making my work and risks visible earlier, which is why I'm (action)]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Fake weaknesses ("perfectionist").
- A weakness that is a core requirement of the role (for example, "I'm weak in Java").
- Three unrelated lists with no common thread.
- Strengths with no evidence.
- Spending more time on strengths than on weaknesses.
