**Short answer:** Give an honest, direct answer that matches your real situation, and say it positively. If you are flexible, say so and add that you'd like to know the timeline and support. If you have a constraint, state it plainly with the reason in one line and say what you *can* do (for example, open to hybrid, or to relocate after a certain date). Never say "no problem" if it is a problem: it will come back at offer time.

## What they are checking

- **Practical fit:** can they place you where the team is? Entain India's tech hub is in Hyderabad, so for most roles this is about Hyderabad and the office or hybrid policy, but ask what they have in mind.
- **Commitment:** will you join and stay, or drop out over location later?
- **Honesty and maturity:** a clear answer, not an evasive one.

## Structure

1. **Direct answer first:** yes, yes with conditions, or a clear constraint.
2. **One line of context** (only if there is a constraint).
3. **What you can offer** (flexibility, a date, hybrid).
4. **A question back**, if useful: "Which location and team do you have in mind?"

## Template

Pick the version that is true for you.

**Fully flexible:**

> "No problem at all. I'm open to **[Hyderabad / any of your locations]**. I'd just like to know the expected joining date and whether there is relocation support, so I can plan the move."

**Flexible with a preference:**

> "I'm open to it. My preference is **[city]** because **[short reason, e.g. I'm already based there]**, but if the role needs me in **[other city]**, I can relocate by **[date / after my notice period]**."

**Real constraint:**

> "I'd like to be upfront: I'm **[constraint, e.g. based in X for family reasons until DATE]**. I can **[what you can do, e.g. work hybrid / travel for N days a month / relocate from DATE]**. Is that compatible with this team's setup?"

**About work model** (if asked about office days instead):

> "I'm comfortable with **[the office policy they described / N days in office]**. I've worked **[remote / hybrid / on-site]** for **[N years]**, so either works for me."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying yes to everything, then negotiating at offer stage.
- A long personal story; one line of reason is enough.
- Sounding reluctant ("I guess I could...") if you are actually fine.
- Assuming the location: ask which office and team.
- Turning it into a salary negotiation in the same breath; raise relocation support as a question, not a condition.
