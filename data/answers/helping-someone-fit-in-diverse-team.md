**Short answer:** Describe someone who was left out (a new joiner from another country or background, a remote member in a different time zone, someone with less fluent English) and the small, concrete things you did to include them. On "team problem or person's problem": it is mainly the team's responsibility, because the team sets the norms that decide who can take part. The person also has a part to play, but the team should make it easy.

## What they are checking

- **Inclusion:** you notice who is on the edge and act.
- **Respect:** you adapt the team's habits, not try to change the person.
- **Concrete actions**, not good intentions.
- **The second question:** a mature, balanced view, leaning on team responsibility.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection, then a direct answer to "whose problem".

Possible actions: speak slower and avoid local slang; move a recurring meeting to a time that works across zones; write decisions down so people who don't speak up in calls can follow; invite them into code reviews and design discussions; ask their opinion directly in meetings; pair on work; learn something about their holidays or customs.

## Template

> "At [company], [person's role] joined from [different country / culture / time zone]. In meetings they [symptom, e.g. rarely spoke], and I saw [example, e.g. decisions were made in quick hallway chats they missed].
>
> I [actions, e.g. started posting a short written summary after each design discussion, asked for their view by name in reviews, and paired with them on their first feature]. I also suggested to the team [norm change, e.g. moving stand-up 30 minutes earlier so it was inside their working day].
>
> After [time], [result, e.g. they were leading discussions on the module they owned, and others said the summaries helped them too].
>
> On whether it's a team or individual problem: mostly the team's. The team sets the norms, the meeting times and the jokes. The person also needs to try, ask questions and share context, but you can't ask someone to adapt to norms they can't see. Fixing the norms helped everyone, not just them."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying "it's the person's job to adapt".
- Stereotyping the person's culture while telling the story.
- Only one gesture (one lunch) with no lasting change.
- Inventing an example. If you have none, describe what you would do.
