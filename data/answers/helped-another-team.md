**Short answer:** Tell a real case where another team had a problem you could help with. Say how you learned of it, how you checked that it was right to spend the time (and told your own team), what you did, and how you left them able to continue without you.

Finish with the result for *them* and for the company. The best stories end with a lasting fix, not a favour repeated every week.

## What they are checking

- **Company before team:** you notice problems outside your box.
- **Judgement:** you helped without dropping your own commitments.
- **Respect:** you worked with the owners, not around them.
- **Leaving things better:** knowledge shared, root cause fixed.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** which team (role only), their problem, why it mattered.
2. **Why you:** the knowledge or access you had.
3. **Agreement:** time limit, your manager informed, their owner still in charge.
4. **Action:** what you did, step by step.
5. **Hand-over:** notes, tests, pairing, a runbook.
6. **Result:** theirs first, then yours.
7. **Reflection:** what you learned about the other side.

Kinds of help that make good stories (use only a true one):
- Debugging an incident where your service was one of the suspects.
- A shared library or script that solved the same problem for both teams.
- Helping a team move to an API or framework you had already adopted.
- Support for security, data or operations: fixing findings, adding logs, preparing an audit.

## Template

> "[Team] was [problem], and it affected [who]. It was not my area: I worked on [your area].
>
> I could help because [knowledge]. I told [my manager] it would take about [time], and moved [task] so that nothing of ours slipped.
>
> I agreed with [their owner] that they stay in charge and I [your role].
>
> I [action 1], which showed [finding]. Then we [action 2] together, so that they could do it next time.
>
> Before stepping back I [hand-over: wrote a page, added a test, showed two people].
>
> The result was [their outcome with a number]. For us, [benefit, e.g. fewer tickets caused by that issue, a better relationship].
>
> I learned [lesson], and now I [habit, e.g. talk to the teams that use our API once a month]."

**If asked "when would you say no?":**

> "When it would break a promise my team has made, or when they need a permanent owner. Then I say so quickly, point to someone who can help, and give what I know in writing."

## Mistakes to avoid

- A story that is really part of your own job.
- Letting your own work slip and hiding it.
- The hero who becomes the permanent helpdesk.
- No result for the other team.
