**Short answer:** This is three questions. *Why Microsoft:* connect a real reason (scale of its developer and cloud platforms, the specific team's product, its growth-mindset culture) to your own direction. *AI tools:* give specific uses, such as Claude Code or Copilot for boilerplate, tests and exploring an unfamiliar codebase, and say how you verify the output. *Non-technical skill:* name a real one and the step you are already taking to improve it.

## What they are checking

- **Motivation:** you know what Microsoft and this team do, beyond the brand.
- **AI maturity:** you get real productivity from AI tools *and* you stay responsible for correctness, security and design.
- **Growth mindset** (a Microsoft culture value): a genuine weakness with an action plan.

## Structure

Three short answers, about 45 seconds each.

1. **Why Microsoft:** one reason about the product or team, one about scale, one about culture or growth. Link each to you.
2. **AI tools:** 2-3 concrete uses, one example with a result, and your guardrails.
3. **Skill to improve:** the skill, the evidence that it matters, what you are doing about it.

## Template

> **Why Microsoft:** "[Team/product] matters to me because [reason]. I've spent ~5 years building Java/Spring Boot services, and I want to work on [scale / platform problem] at Microsoft's scale. The growth-mindset culture also fits how I like to work: [brief example]."
>
> **AI tools:** "I use [Claude Code / GitHub Copilot] daily. Mostly for:
> - writing boilerplate and test cases, which I then review line by line;
> - understanding unfamiliar code, by asking it to trace a request through a module;
> - first drafts of SQL or migration scripts, which I check with EXPLAIN and on test data.
>
> On [project], it helped me [specific result, e.g. write the test suite for X in a day instead of three]. My rules: I never merge code I can't explain, I don't paste secrets or customer data into it, and I treat its suggestions on concurrency or security as a starting point to verify, not an answer."
>
> **Skill to improve:** "[Skill, e.g. presenting designs to non-technical stakeholders]. I noticed it when [evidence]. I'm working on it by [action, e.g. writing a one-paragraph summary before every design review, and volunteering to present our sprint demo]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- "Microsoft is a big company with good benefits."
- Saying you don't use AI tools at all, or that you accept output without review.
- A fake weakness ("I work too hard").
- Spending all your time on one of the three parts.
