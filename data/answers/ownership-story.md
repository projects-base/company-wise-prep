**Short answer:** Choose a problem that nobody had assigned to you, or a task you carried far past its description, through release and after. Show that you noticed it, decided it mattered, involved the right people, saw it through the boring parts, and checked later that it stayed fixed.

Ownership is being responsible for the *outcome*, not doing everything yourself. Give the number that proves the outcome.

## What they are checking

- **Initiative:** you acted without being told.
- **End to end:** design, build, release, monitoring, follow-up.
- **Judgement:** it was worth doing, and you did not step on others.
- **Scope:** for staff level, a problem wider than your own team.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** the problem, why nobody owned it, the cost of leaving it.
2. **Decision:** why you picked it up, and who you told.
3. **Action:** the plan, the hard part, the people you brought in.
4. **After release:** monitoring, documentation, hand-over.
5. **Result:** a number, some months later if possible.
6. **Reflection.**

Signs of ownership to show, if they are true:
- You found the real cause instead of closing the ticket.
- You wrote the runbook, the alert or the test nobody asked for.
- You said "this was my mistake" and fixed the process.

Stories that fit (choose a real one): a flaky job everyone restarted by hand, a service with no owner after a reorganisation, a recurring support ticket, a slow build, a migration stuck at 90%, an incident followed to its last action.

## Template

> "[Problem] had been happening for [time]. It sat between [team A] and [team B], so nobody owned it, and it cost [impact].
>
> It was not my task, but [reason it mattered]. I told [manager] that I wanted to take it, what it would need, and what I would put aside.
>
> I started by [finding the facts]. The real cause was [cause], not [what people assumed].
>
> I [plan and key decision]. The hardest part was [obstacle], and I dealt with it by [action, people involved].
>
> I released it [safely: flag, canary], added [alert, test, document], and [one thing that went wrong and how you handled it].
>
> [Period] later, [metric] was [after], compared with [before]. [Team] runs it now without me.
>
> What I would do differently is [reflection]."

Likely follow-ups:
- *Did you ask for permission?* I informed early, and asked where it affected another team's system or priorities.
- *What if the owner had objected?* I would have offered help on their terms, and raised the cost with both managers if it stayed stuck.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Your normal assigned work told as ownership.
- A lone hero who tells nobody and works nights.
- Taking over someone's area without talking to them.
- Ending at "deployed".
- Only "we": say what you decided and did.
