**Short answer:** Tell a real case where your mentoring approach did not work, for example too much hand-holding, too little structure, or advice that did not fit the person. Own your part, explain how you noticed it, what you changed, and what you do differently now as a mentor. The point is the lesson, not the junior's failings.

## What they are checking

- **Growth mindset** (a Microsoft value): you can learn from a miss.
- **Self-awareness as a mentor:** you look at your method first.
- **Adaptability:** different people need different mentoring.
- **Respect:** you don't blame the junior.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

Common, honest mentoring mistakes that make good stories:
- **Giving answers instead of guidance:** they finished tasks but did not learn, and stayed dependent.
- **Assuming knowledge:** you skipped basics (how Spring wires beans, how transactions work) and they were too shy to say so.
- **One-size-fits-all:** your style suited you, not them.
- **No clear goals:** sessions happened, but nothing measurable changed.
- **Unclear feedback:** you were too gentle and the problem persisted.

## Template

> "I was mentoring [a junior / new joiner] on [team / area]. My approach was [what you did, e.g. whenever they were stuck, I'd jump in and fix it quickly].
>
> After [time], I noticed [sign, e.g. they still couldn't debug a failing integration test alone, and in our sprint review they couldn't explain code they had shipped]. Looking back, my approach was the problem: [your part, e.g. I optimised for closing tickets, not for their learning].
>
> I changed it: [actions, e.g. I started asking 'what have you tried?' first, we set two learning goals for the quarter, and I let them drive during pairing while I only asked questions].
>
> It took [time], and [result, e.g. they started resolving most issues alone and reviewed others' PRs]. Not everything worked: [an honest remaining gap, optional].
>
> Now, with anyone I mentor, I [habit, e.g. agree on goals in the first week and ask how they prefer to learn]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- Blaming the mentee ("they just weren't motivated").
- A story where it actually went well ("it was bad at first but then perfect").
- No concrete change in your behaviour.
- Picking a trivial case (one awkward meeting).
