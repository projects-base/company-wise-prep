**Short answer:** Pick one person and one clear change: where they started, what they could do alone at the end, and what you did in between. Describe your method (a goal, small real tasks, questions before answers, honest feedback), and one moment where you adjusted to how they learn.

Measure it by *their* independence, not by how much you helped. Say what you learned too.

## What they are checking

- **You make others better**, which is expected at senior level.
- **Method:** more than "I answered their questions".
- **Patience and adaptation:** different people need different help.
- **Feedback:** you can say a hard thing kindly.
- **Balance:** you still delivered your own work.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** who (role only), their level, why they needed support.
2. **Goal:** agreed *with* them, in terms of something they will be able to do.
3. **Action:** a plan of growing tasks, pairing, review, feedback.
4. **Adjustment:** what did not work, and what you changed.
5. **Result:** what they do alone now.
6. **Reflection:** what it taught you.

A ladder you can describe, from most help to least:

1. **I do, you watch:** think aloud while debugging.
2. **We do:** pair, they type.
3. **You do, I review:** a small real task with a clear end.
4. **You own:** a feature, including its release and its alerts.
5. **You teach:** they explain it to the next person.

Habits worth mentioning if they are yours:
- "What have you tried, and what do you think is happening?" before answering.
- Praise in public, correct in private.

## Template

> "[Person, role] joined [team] with [background]. They struggled with [specific thing], and [effect on them or the team].
>
> We agreed a goal: within [time], they would [do X alone]. I started with [first step, e.g. pairing on two bugs], then gave them [small task], then [larger task].
>
> At first I [approach that did not fit], and I noticed [sign]. I asked what would help, and changed to [new approach].
>
> I also had to tell them [difficult feedback]. I did it by [how], and they [reaction].
>
> After [time], they [evidence of independence: owned a feature, led a release, reviewed others]. My own work [how you protected it].
>
> I learned [lesson], and with the next person I [change]."

Likely follow-ups:
- *What if they do not improve?* Make the gap specific, check the cause (skill, clarity, confidence, workload), agree a short plan with dates, and involve the manager early and openly.
- *How do you balance it with delivery?* Fixed times, and their tasks are real work, not extra work.

## Mistakes to avoid

- "I mentor everyone" with no single story.
- Describing the person as weak or lazy.
- Doing the work for them and calling it mentoring.
- No evidence that they changed.
