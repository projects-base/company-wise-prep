**Short answer:** Pick a real technical disagreement, ideally about a design, an approach or a priority, with a colleague or your manager. Show that you understood their reasoning first, moved the discussion to shared goals and evidence (data, a quick prototype, agreed criteria), and accepted the outcome even if it was not your idea. Finish with the result and what you learned about working with that person.

## What they are checking

Google asks this for Googleyness; Microsoft for respect and collaboration.

- **Respect:** you disagree without making it personal.
- **Listening:** you can state the other person's view fairly.
- **Evidence over opinion:** you settle it with data or experiments.
- **Disagree and commit:** you support the final decision.
- **Outcome:** the relationship and the project both came out fine.

## Structure

STAR + Reflection.

1. **Situation:** who (role only), what was being decided, why it mattered.
2. **Both positions:** yours and theirs, described fairly.
3. **Action:** private conversation, shared goal, criteria, evidence, a decision owner.
4. **Result:** what was decided, and whether it worked.
5. **Reflection.**

Good topics: sync REST vs async messaging between services, adding a cache vs fixing the query, a big-bang vs incremental migration, shipping now vs fixing tech debt, how strict code review should be.

## Template

> "On [project], [colleague / manager] and I disagreed about [decision]. I wanted [your option] because [reason]. They wanted [their option] because [their reason, stated fairly].
>
> It started to slow the team down, so I suggested we [step, e.g. agree the criteria first: latency, effort, risk]. I [evidence, e.g. built a small benchmark / pulled production metrics], which showed [finding].
>
> In the end we went with [decision]. [If it wasn't yours: I committed to it fully and helped implement (part).] [If it was yours: I made sure their concern about (X) was addressed by (Y).]
>
> The result was [outcome]. What I learned was [reflection, e.g. agreeing on criteria before debating options ends most arguments quickly]. We [relationship result, e.g. work well together, and I now ask for their review on designs early]."

## Mistakes to avoid

- A story where the other person is just wrong or difficult.
- An emotional or personality conflict with no technical substance.
- "I won" as the main point.
- Escalating to the manager as the first move.
- No outcome or no reflection.
